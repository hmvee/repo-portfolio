## Brief

Orchard Co. is a small Edinburgh-based food business that delivers weekly boxes
of organic produce from farms within 50 miles of the city. They'd been operating
for three years with a visual identity that one of the founders had put together
in Canva, and they were ready to invest in something that felt more intentional.

The brief was to create a visual identity that communicated honesty and quality
without tipping into the kind of rustic-artisanal cliché that saturates the
organic food market. They didn't want a logo that looked like it had a farmhouse
and a weathervane in it.

## Process

I spent the first engagement exploring references with the founders — not food
brands, but things like scientific illustration, mid-century grocery packaging,
and Scandinavian print design from the 1970s. The direction that emerged was:
clear and factual, with warmth coming from the illustration style rather than
from nostalgic cues.

The wordmark uses a modified geometric sans — not a display typeface, but not
a corporate one either. The idea was that the company name would do the work
of communicating what they do, and the logotype would stand out by being
quieter than everything else in the category.

## What was delivered

**Logomark and wordmark** — Primary and secondary lockups, rules for clear space
and minimum sizes, approved color combinations.

**Color system** — Five colors anchored around a warm ochre, with supporting earth
tones and a clean off-white. All pairs meet WCAG AA contrast for any text use.

**Typography** — Two typefaces: a geometric sans for UI and headers, and a
humanist serif for running text and longer print pieces.

**Illustration style guide** — Six original botanical illustrations and a style
guide specifying linework weight, color fills, and composition principles so a
future illustrator could extend the system without my involvement.

**Packaging templates** — Dielines and specifications for the weekly box, the
smaller specialty boxes, and the paper tape used to seal them.

**Print collateral** — Letterhead, cards, and a one-page supplier fact sheet template.

## Outcome

The rebrand launched in spring 2023. The founders reported an increase in box
subscriptions in the three months following launch, which they attributed partly
to improved word-of-mouth driven by people photographing and sharing their boxes.

I'm not in the business of claiming credit for business outcomes — a lot of
factors were at play. What I can say is that the packaging works as packaging:
it's unambiguous, distinctive on a doorstep, and gives the impression that someone
cared about the details. Which they did.
